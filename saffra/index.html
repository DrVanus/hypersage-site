A real chef persona — not a generic LLM

Powered by advanced AI — shaped into a real chef. Saffra has a name, a voice, signature moves, and hard safety rules. Her tone calibrates to your skill: beginners get sensory cues ("when oil shimmers, when it smells nutty"), pros get terse technique-first ("hit it with a nappé"). One model, infinite cooks.

A catalog built from sources — not improvised at the stove

No flagship recipe Saffra knows was written in one pass. Each dish is cross-referenced across tested, editor-vetted sources with a trust weight on every one; ingredients are scored core, variation or outlier; three AI chef personas — authenticity, weeknight ease, technique — then draft it independently from that consensus, and the merged draft is run through a food-safety and allergen validation pass before it ships. Saffra's catalog: ~900 flagship recipes across 25+ cuisines, plus a 230,000-recipe semantic search index for breadth (vector embeddings). Then she adapts it to your pantry, your skill, your equipment.

Memory that compounds

Rolling conversation summaries. Cook history with star ratings. Live pantry awareness. Recent-suggestion tracking so she doesn't pitch the same dish twice. The longer you cook with her, the sharper she gets — leaning into what you loved, steering around what flopped, varying within genres you've succeeded at, never repeating last Tuesday's dinner unless you ask.

Nine specialized tools, used silently

Saffra doesn't just talk — she works. She has nine purpose-built tools: pantry search, recipe lookup, recipe adaptation, ingredient substitution, technique explainer, voice timers, unit conversion, recipe saving, profile updates. She picks the right tools for each turn and chains them — "what's for dinner?" might fire pantry-search → recipe-lookup → adapt-for-user, all silently before answering.

Allergen safety as a hard rule

Declared allergies are non-negotiable — hard refusal rules, not soft suggestions. She'll either swap loud and clear or pass on the recipe entirely. Cross-contamination flags for serious allergies (peanut, tree nut, shellfish, sesame, gluten-for-celiac). USDA-safe internal temperatures cited in every recipe that needs one.

A voice built for a real kitchen

Streaming neural TTS — Saffra's signature voice — served from a content-addressed cache so the same line never re-renders twice. Speech recognition runs on-device via Apple's Speech framework — your voice never leaves the phone unless on-device accuracy fails. The wake phrase "hey saffra" is only armed inside Cooking Mode, never app-wide. Voice timers land in Live Activities, including the Dynamic Island.

Cooking Mode

One step at a time.
Hands never touch the screen.

Tap "Let's cook" on any recipe and the whole phone becomes a chef at your shoulder. One step per screen, big enough to read from across the counter, with a photo of the dish, the exact ingredients this step needs, and a timer she's already sized for you.

  • She reads every step aloud

    Saffra narrates in her signature voice, then listens. "Hey saffra, next step" — "replay that" — "how long on the timer?" Your hands stay in the food.

  • Just the ingredients this step needs

    Each step card pulls out exactly what you're reaching for — "spaghetti, salt" — so you're never re-scanning the full list mid-sizzle.

  • Timers she's already sized

    "15 min — saffra suggests this timer." One tap (or one sentence) and it's running in the Dynamic Island, even if you leave the app.

  • Ask anything, mid-step

    "Why reserve the pasta water?" The ask-saffra bar answers inline without leaving the step — and a "coming up" preview keeps you one move ahead.

Your Kitchen

A pantry that fills itself —
and empties smart.

Saffra can only cook from your kitchen if she knows what's in it — so getting food into the app takes seconds, not typing. And once it's in, freshness is tracked: things about to turn get flagged and pitched for tonight's dinner instead of the trash.

  • Snap your fridge

    Photograph a shelf and she identifies what's on it. Items land categorized, no typing. This one photo is sent to our server to be read; receipts and cookbook pages are read on your phone.

  • Scan a receipt

    On-device OCR reads the lines, AI parses them into clean items — your photo never leaves the phone.

  • Or beep the barcode

    Lookup runs against Open Food Facts — items arrive pre-tagged with category, expiration, and allergen flags.

  • Expiring? It's dinner.

    Items going stale get flagged in the pantry and surfaced in chat — "the cilantro's going yellow, let's use it tonight."

Search

get dinner.

Search runs on vector embeddings across 230,000+ recipes — so "cozy noodle soup" and "something bright with lemon" work as well as exact titles. Every result comes back as a photo card with time and cuisine up front, and refinements for time, diet, and cuisine are one tap away.

  • Meaning, not keywords

    Semantic search understands the dish you're describing — no more guessing the magic word a recipe site indexed.

  • Filters that matter

    Under 30 minutes. Vegetarian. Thai. Stack refinements and the results re-rank instantly.

  • Flagship first

    Flagship recipes from the consensus-built catalog rank above the wider index, so the top results are the ones that went through the full pipeline.

Three differences matter. First: a general model may draft a recipe from model knowledge alone. Saffra's flagship recipes are built before you ask for them: the dish is cross-referenced across tested, editor-vetted sources, three AI chef personas draft from that consensus, and the merged draft receives food-safety and allergen-completeness checks. Second: the assistant works with your pantry, equipment, allergies, skill level, and current cooking step rather than treating each question in isolation. Third: product-specific tools handle catalog search, substitutions, techniques, and hands-free cooking, so the model is one part of a larger cooking system.

The core assistant uses a multimodal AI model, with a faster utility model for tasks such as parsing receipt text and folding older conversations into summaries. Recipe search uses vector embeddings over the curated library. Voice synthesis uses a neural TTS voice with content-addressed caching, while speech recognition runs on-device through Apple's Speech framework with an OpenAI Whisper fallback when on-device accuracy is insufficient. Receipt and cookbook OCR runs on-device through Apple's Vision framework; shelf photos are sent to the server for vision analysis and are not stored. The recipe catalog is built ahead of time through a multi-source consensus pipeline with food-safety and allergen checks.

Two things keep her grounded. One: when you ask for a specific dish or "what should I cook?", she calls a tool that searches our curated recipe library — hand-curated flagship recipes plus 230,000 CC0-licensed recipes — instead of improvising one from memory. So you get a real recipe, adapted for you, not a hallucination. Two: for techniques and substitutions she calls dedicated tools (technique explainer, substitution lookup) rather than guessing. She also won't claim to have personally tasted something — she'll say "by the numbers, this should read bright and savory" instead of "I love this." If she's not sure, she says so.
