A chef with her own style

Saffra has a name, a voice, signature moves, and hard safety rules. She pitches her answers to your skill. Beginners get sensory cues ("when oil shimmers, when it smells nutty"). Pros get the short version ("reduce to nappé").

A catalog written before you ask

2,300+ recipes across 25+ cuisines, each flagged for allergens and searchable by what you're craving. 99.8% of ingredients carry a measured amount, 97.2% say how to prep it, every recipe lists the equipment it needs, and 99.3% name a cue for what done looks like. Then she adapts the one you pick to your pantry, your skill, and your equipment.

She remembers what you've cooked

Your cook history, your star ratings, and your pantry all shape what she suggests next. She leans into what you loved, steers around what flopped, and won't bring back last Tuesday's dinner unless you ask.

Eleven kitchen tools

Ask "what's for dinner?" and she might check your pantry, find a recipe, and adapt it for you, one tool after another. Her kit covers pantry search, recipe lookup, recipe adaptation, recipe saving, timers, substitutions, technique explainer, troubleshooting, pairings, nutrition lookup, and unit conversion.

Allergies are hard rules

If a recipe touches a declared allergy, she swaps the ingredient and tells you, or passes on the dish. For serious allergies (peanut, tree nut, shellfish, sesame, gluten for celiac) she also flags cross-contamination risks, and she gives target internal temperatures where they matter.

A voice for a busy kitchen

Saffra speaks in a natural-sounding AI voice. In Cooking Mode she listens for "hey saffra", and voice timers show up on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.

Cooking Mode

One step at a time.
Your hands stay in the food.

Tap "Let's cook" on any recipe and your phone becomes a chef at your shoulder. One step per screen, big enough to read from across the counter, with a photo of the dish and the ingredients that step needs.

  • She reads every step aloud

    Saffra narrates in her signature voice, then listens. "Hey saffra, next step." "Replay that." "How long on the timer?"

  • Just the ingredients this step needs

    Each step card pulls out what you're reaching for ("spaghetti, salt"), so you're not re-scanning the full list mid-sizzle.

  • Timers she's already sized

    "15 min — saffra suggests this timer." One tap (or one sentence) and it's running in the Dynamic Island, even if you leave the app.

  • Ask anything, mid-step

    "Why reserve the pasta water?" Ask without leaving the step. A "coming up" preview keeps you one move ahead.

FAQ

Common questions.

A general chatbot writes a recipe from scratch the moment you ask. Saffra's catalog recipes are written in advance and complete enough to cook from: 99.8% of ingredients carry a measured amount, 97.2% say how to prep it, every recipe lists the equipment it needs, and 99.3% name a cue for what done looks like. Unlike a recipe site, she knows your pantry, equipment, allergies, skill level, and the step you're on, and she has dedicated tools for search, substitutions, techniques, and hands-free cooking.

The core assistant uses a multimodal AI model, with a faster utility model for tasks such as parsing receipt text and condensing the older part of a long chat. Recipe search uses vector embeddings over the curated library. Her voice is a natural-sounding AI voice, and lines she has spoken before are kept ready to replay, while speech recognition uses Apple's Speech framework, which is server-backed — the audio reaches Apple, not us. In Cooking Mode the clip is also re-transcribed by OpenAI's Whisper, for every spoken question rather than only noisy ones, then discarded. Receipt text is read on-device through Apple's Vision framework; shelf photos are sent to the server for vision analysis and are not stored. The recipe catalog is written ahead of time rather than improvised per request, and every recipe carries allergen flags.

Ask for a dish or "what should I cook?" and she searches Saffra's own catalog, where every recipe is written in advance, then adapts what she finds for you. For techniques and substitutions she uses dedicated tools. She won't claim to have tasted anything: she'll say "by the numbers, this should read bright and savory" instead of "I love this." If she's not sure, she says so.

Saffra covers cuisines including Thai, Mexican regional, Korean, Vietnamese, French, Italian regional, Chinese regional, West African, Levantine, and Peruvian, and dietary needs including vegan, keto, halal, kosher, FODMAP, Whole30, and celiac. She cooks for first-timers and technique-focused home cooks alike, in kitchens from induction-only or no-oven setups to fully equipped ones. For dishes outside the catalog, she draws on regional and technique knowledge and tells you when she's unsure.
