Our intelligence layer

The model is only one part.

Our companion products do not begin each turn with an empty chat box. They assemble the right context, carry continuity on purpose, apply rules the domain can defend, and return the result through an interface built for the task.

  1. Your record

    Goals, preferences, conditions, boundaries, and anything else you choose to share.

  2. Recent activity

    Diary entries, symptoms, check-ins, cooking history, conversations, and other product events.

  3. The current task

    Where the product supports it: the open recipe, selected person, story chapter, or live market state.

  4. A product-specific result

    Context is combined with the relevant domain data, tools, rules, and interface.

Context

The app sends the situation, not just the sentence

Every companion rebuilds a structured context package before it answers: what the person has told it, what they have logged, and what the conversation already established. That can mean symptoms and medications, diary entries and check-ins, pantry and allergies, a selected person and saved screenshots, or consented holdings and live market data. The new message is one input among many.

Continuity

Memory with a boundary

Continuity is represented explicitly: bounded facts, editable profiles, recent activity, and thread history. The product decides what should last, what should be replaced, and what can be cleared instead of asking a model to improvise memory from an endless transcript. Storage, sync, and controls are designed per product.

Domain logic

Domain knowledge is designed into the product

A general model can draft. The product supplies task-specific structure: schemas, reference data, tools, boundaries, and response formats. A chef works from pantry, allergies, recipes, and the current step; a portfolio assistant works from consented holdings, watchlists, and live markets. In the health, recovery, and DBT companions, separate safety checks can surface urgent resources before generation and before message limits. It is not one generic chatbot with a new name and colour.

App-native delivery

The answer keeps the shape of the work

An answer can become a structured symptom entry, an appointment brief, an adapted recipe, a character memory, a narrated scene, or the next action in a workflow. Streamed replies preserve the reader's place rather than pulling them away from earlier text, with stop controls and voice support where the product provides them. Chat is an interface; the product is everything that happens around it.

The portfolio uses Anthropic and OpenAI models; the exact provider and data path vary by product and feature.

Product discipline

Shared foundations. Product-specific judgment.

Reuse matters when it carries good mechanisms forward without flattening the product. The context, rules, interface, and operating boundaries are reconsidered for every job.

The premise

Useful AI comes from the system around the model.

Models are capable generalists. Products constrain them to specific work by deciding what context enters, what tools are available, which claims are out of bounds, and what happens when the system reaches a boundary. The model matters; the product design around it matters more.

Fit the work

Start with the task, not the model

We define what the product must know, what it must check, and how the result should behave before deciding which model call belongs in the loop. A useful workflow is the specification; chat is only one possible interface.

Show the boundary

Say what the system knows — and what it does not

Provider, data path, memory, limits, and the role of the AI belong in the product experience and its product page. High-stakes claims are scoped to the products that can support them. Those boundaries belong in the product design and disclosure from the beginning.

Reuse carefully

Carry mechanisms forward, not assumptions

Context assembly, memory stores, streaming behaviour, voice fallback, and safety patterns move between products when they fit. Each adoption is re-checked against the new domain. Shared architecture should compound learning without creating copy-and-paste products.

Direct ownership

The people building it read the feedback

Product questions and reported problems reach the studio directly, keeping the path from a user observation to a changed interface, model behaviour, or policy short. That short path makes feedback easier to trace into product decisions.

The studio

Small enough to stay close to the details.

Hypersage is an independent consumer-product studio working across health, relationships, cooking, finance, stories, and play. Each category forces a different answer to the same question: what would make intelligence genuinely useful here?

We use AI where it improves the product and ordinary software where it does not. The goal is not to put a chatbot everywhere. It is to make each product understand its job.

A cooking companion, a story reader, and a game should not share a voice or visual system. They should share disciplined decisions underneath. If that approach resonates, talk to the studio.

  • Context before conversation The product prepares the situation before the model sees the latest message.
  • Domain depth over generic chat Schemas, data, tools, and task-specific checks are designed for the specific job.
  • Clear boundaries The system states what it knows, what it sends, and what it cannot decide.
  • Craft throughout Interface, illustration, motion, and — where it matters — voice are authored for the product.
