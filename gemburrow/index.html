      How it plays

      Grabbing is easy. Digging is the game.

      Five orders hang on a line above the jar. Every one wants a specific set of gems, and you only get paid when a set is complete — so the jar stops being a pile of treasure and starts being a plan.

      The satchel

      Seven slots. Gems you dig go in the bag and stay there until they finish an order. Fill it with things nobody asked for and the next gem you pull is scrap — the pressure sits on every single tap, not on rare mistakes.

      55 swings

      One swing per gem or rock, and that's the whole shift. No clock, because a pile of physics settles at its own pace on its own phone — but spam the top layer and you'll run out of pickaxe before you run out of orders.

      The pile answers back

      Pull a stone from the middle and everything above it collapses into the gap, exposing gems that weren't reachable a second ago. Rocks pay a coin and cost a swing — sometimes clearing one is the whole move.

      Orders that refill

      Deliver a set and a fresh card drops onto the line in its place. A Rush order arrives with a 45-second dial and pays for the hurry. Throughput against payout is the economy of a shift.

      Heartstones and geodes

      Some rocks crack open into live gems. And buried deep in the biggest jars is a Heartstone — a colossal gem that takes three pries to free, then asks whether it's worth 600 coins or worth keeping.

      It rings in tune

      Every combo ladder is harmonised to the chord the soundtrack is actually playing, and the fanfare scales with what you actually earned. A shift that lost money never gets a celebration.

      The collection

      Use it… or feed the dragon.

      Hoardling sleeps on a ledge in the shop. When you unearth a rare prism and an order happens to want it, the game stops and asks on two labelled plates: USE IT, into your satchel toward that card, or HOARD, and Hoardling keeps it forever. You get six seconds, and if you don't answer the game answers for you — into the satchel while a card still wants it, otherwise to the dragon. A rare is never scrapped and a payout is never quietly taken from you. Only the Heartstone, the colossus buried near the bottom, is ever actually sold.

      The deal

      An honest game about greed.

      Gemburrow is about hoarding treasure. It would be a poor joke to build that on a slot machine, so we didn't: there is one optional purchase in the whole game, nothing you can buy changes a dig, and nothing stops you playing.

      Common questions

      FAQ

      When can I play Gemburrow?

      Soon. Gemburrow is coming to iPhone and isn't on the App Store yet. If you'd like a note the day the shop opens, email support@hypersage.ai and we'll tell you at launch. You do not have to wait to play, though: the whole game runs free in your browser at hypersage.ai/gemburrow/play/, and you can add it to your home screen.

      Is this the game from those gem-jar ads?

      It's the game those ads were pretending to be. That jar-full-of-gems clip has run against a dozen different apps, and downloading any of them gets you something else entirely. Gemburrow is that clip built as the actual game: a jar packed with gems and rocks, real physics, and orders to fill by digging the right ones out.

      Does it cost anything, and are there ads?

      Gemburrow is free to play, and there is one optional in-app purchase: The Full Burrow, a single one-time unlock that opens career levels 11–40 and the 14-day dig archive. Everything else is free forever — free digs, the Daily Dig with its leaderboard and league, career levels 1–10, all 27 contracts, and the whole shop and wardrobe. That one unlock is the entire catalogue: no subscription, no coin packs, no advertising SDK and no energy meter. The coins you spend in the mole's shop are earned by digging and are not for sale. The browser version at hypersage.ai/gemburrow/play/ carries no store at all, so the whole 40-level ladder is open there.

      What do coins actually do?

      They bank between shifts and buy dig walls in the mole's shop — six of them, from Burrow Clay up to Deep Basalt. Those are purely cosmetic on purpose: the Daily Dig has to be the same jar and the same seven satchel slots for everybody, so nothing you can buy is allowed to change how a dig plays. Coins also pay for a Deeper Pick, which adds eight swings to one retry of a career level you're stuck on.

      What is the Daily Dig?

      One jar a day, identical for every player in the world — same gems, same rocks, same buried prisms — and a leaderboard of the day's best hauls. It resets at midnight UTC. Posting a score sends only a random ID made on your device, an auto-generated name like MOLE-4F2A, and the run's numbers; there is no account and no sign-in anywhere in the game.

      Can I play without a connection?

      Yes — Career and Free digs make no network calls at all, and your progress is stored on your phone. The Daily Dig plays offline too; only posting your score and drawing the leaderboard need a connection, and a score dug offline is queued and sent the next time the game opens online.

      How long is a shift, and is there a timer?

      There is no session clock, deliberately — a pile of physics takes as long as it takes to settle, and that is different on every phone, so billing you for it would be unfair. What limits a shift is the pickaxe: 55 swings, one per gem or rock you pull. Individual Rush orders do carry their own 45-second countdown, shown as a dial on the card.

      Is the art hand-painted?

      No — it's AI-generated, and we'd rather say so than pretend. Every gem, rock, the mole and the dragon come from an AI image model working under one painterly art direction, then get curated and repaired until the whole set holds together. The music is composed and rendered in-house by our own script — no stock art, no licensed audio.
