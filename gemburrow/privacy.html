Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 14, 2026

The short version. Gemburrow has no account, no analytics and no advertising SDK. The iPhone app has one in-app purchase, and Apple runs it: we never see or store a card, a name or a billing address. Your progress lives on your device. The one thing that ever leaves it is a Daily Dig score — a random ID made on your device, an auto-generated name like MOLE-4F2A, and the numbers from that run.

What is stored on your device

Gemburrow saves your progress locally: in the iPhone app's storage, or in your browser's local storage when you play at hypersage.ai/gemburrow/play/. That save contains:

  • your coin balance, and which dig walls you have bought and are wearing;
  • your career level and the stars earned on each level;
  • your hoard total — the gems you have given to the dragon;
  • your best Daily Dig and Free Dig results;
  • whether you have seen the first-run coaching, and your sound settings;
  • whether the Full Burrow unlock has been purchased, and a one-way hash of the transaction id it was granted against — so the same purchase is never granted twice. The hash is not the receipt and cannot be turned back into one;
  • a random identifier and the display name generated from it (below).

This save is not sent to us. Deleting the iPhone app deletes it, and so does clearing your browser's data for this site. The app's save may be included in your own iCloud or iTunes device backup, which is Apple's backup of your phone, not a service of ours.

The random identifier

The first time Gemburrow runs it generates a random UUID on the device and builds a display name from its first four characters — for example MOLE-4F2A. That identifier exists for exactly one purpose: so the Daily Dig leaderboard can recognise a repeat submission from the same device and keep only your best score for the day.

It is not your Apple ID, your advertising identifier, or any device identifier issued by Apple, and it is not tied to your name, email address or Apple ID — the game never has any of those. It is, though, the key your Daily Dig scores are stored under: every score posted from one install carries the same identifier and MOLE- name, so the board can tell those scores belong together. It is not shared with anyone else and it is not used to track you across apps or websites. Deleting the iPhone app, or clearing your browser's data for this site, discards it; the game then generates a new one.

Daily Dig scores and leaderboard reads

When you finish a Daily Dig, the game posts that run to our leaderboard, hosted on Supabase. The message contains only:

  • the day the jar belongs to, and that day's shared jar seed;
  • your auto-generated MOLE- name and the random identifier above;
  • the run's results — coins earned and pieces extracted.

Opening a leaderboard fetches its scores. If a Daily score cannot be sent, the game holds it on your device and retries on launch, reconnection or resume while that Daily is still current. Pending scores expire at the next UTC day. Career, Free Dig and archive results are not uploaded. A pending Daily score may retry while another mode is open. No individual tap timings, session logs or crash reports are sent.

Scores are stored for the leaderboard and are visible to other players as a name and a number. If you would like a score row removed, email support@hypersage.ai with the MOLE- name shown on your results screen and we will delete it.

What Gemburrow does not do

  • No account. There is no sign-in, no email, no profile, and no password anywhere in the game.
  • No analytics or crash SDK. None is linked into the build. We do not know how many levels you cleared or when you played.
  • No advertising. No ad SDK, no ad identifier, no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No payment data. The iPhone app has one in-app purchase and no subscription, and the browser version has no store at all. If you buy it, the purchase is handled entirely by the App Store: we never see or receive your card, your Apple ID, your name or your billing address, and none of it is stored in the game or sent anywhere by it.
  • No location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera. The game never asks for any of them.

Children's privacy

Gemburrow is a cozy puzzle game suitable for a wide audience and is not directed at children under 13 specifically. It asks no one, of any age, for a name, an email address or any contact detail. The only data it sends is the Daily Dig score described above, and a player cannot type any text into it — the leaderboard name is generated by the game and cannot be edited, precisely so no personal detail can end up on a public board.

This website

This page and the rest of hypersage.ai/gemburrow are static files served by GitHub Pages. There is no analytics script, no cookie set by us, no advertising pixel and no form. Web fonts are requested from Google Fonts, which receives the request as any font host would, and the game's own files are served by GitHub Pages, which sees those requests the same way any host would.

There are two playable things here, and they do not behave the same way. The small jar demo on the home page runs entirely in your browser, sends nothing anywhere, and keeps no state between visits. The full game at hypersage.ai/gemburrow/play/ behaves exactly like the app described above: it saves your progress in your own browser's local storage, and if you play the Daily Dig it sends that one score — the day and its jar seed, the run's numbers, a generated name like MOLE-4F2A and the random identifier above — to the leaderboard. Nothing else leaves the page, and clearing your browser's data for this site erases the save and the identifier.

Changes to this policy

If the game's data behaviour changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top moves. Wherever the iPhone app is listed on the App Store, material changes will also be reflected in the privacy summary on that listing.
