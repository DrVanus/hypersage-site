Someone to talk to at 3am.

Recovery is mostly the hours nobody sees — the craving at 2:40 in the morning, the day after a slip, the week where nothing feels like progress. Alder is a private companion for those hours: it counts your sober time, sits with you through an urge, and never makes you feel small for having one.

Need help right now?

You don't need this app, an account, or anything else to reach a person. These lines are free, they're staffed by humans, and several of them are open all night. Tap any number to call it.

  • 911 — medical emergency or overdose; ask for naloxone. Many US states have Good Samaritan laws for overdose calls — what they cover varies, and making the call is what matters.
  • 988 — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text, US)
  • 1-800-484-3731 — Never Use Alone, if you're using alone
  • 1-800-662-4357 — SAMHSA National Helpline, free and confidential, 24/7
  • findtreatment.gov — find local care
  • 1-800-GAMBLER — for gambling

These numbers are for the United States. Alder is not an emergency service and no one at Alder sees your messages in real time — if someone is in danger, call.

How it works

Not a streak app. A companion.

Counting days is easy to build and easy to resent. Alder counts them, but the point is what happens on the days the number is about to break.

  1. Say what you're stepping away from

    Alcohol, a substance, vaping, gambling, whatever it is — pick one or several. Each gets its own clock, because progress in one place doesn't have to erase progress in another.

  2. Check in when you can, reach out when you can't

    A short daily check-in takes under a minute. When an urge hits instead, Alder is there — at 3am, on a holiday, in a parking lot — to talk it down rather than tell you to wait.

  3. If it breaks, you get back up

    A lapse starts a debrief, not a lecture: what happened, what came before it, what you'd want next time. The clock restarts, and your total time across every attempt keeps counting — because it happened.

The part most apps get wrong

A lapse is information, not a verdict.

Most trackers treat the day you slip as the day you failed — the number goes to zero, the confetti stops, and the app becomes something you avoid. That's precisely the moment you most need to open it.

  • Alder never shames a lapse. That isn't a tone preference; it's a rule the product is tested against.
  • The debrief asks useful questions — what led up to it, what it cost, what you'd want to happen next time.
  • Your total time keeps counting across every attempt. Eighty-seven days happened whether or not day eighty-eight did.
  • Then it points outward — to the people you listed, or to help that isn't an app.

Being straight with you

What Alder is — and what it isn't.

Recovery is a field full of people promising more than they can deliver. Here is the line, drawn plainly.

Alder does

  • Keep your sober time, across one thing or several
  • Stay up with you while an urge crests and passes
  • Give you a place to log what happened, without judgment
  • Help you get back up after a lapse and keep going
  • Point you toward your people, meetings, and real help
  • Keep the crisis numbers reachable, always, for free

Alder does not

  • Provide therapy, counseling, or clinical care of any kind
  • Diagnose anything, or tell you how severe your situation is
  • Give detox, tapering, or dosing guidance — ever, for anything
  • Discourage medication, meetings, or professional care
  • Replace a person — it is not a stand-in for the humans in your life
  • Watch over you: no one sees your messages in real time

One more thing worth saying plainly: medication for opioid or alcohol use — buprenorphine, methadone, naltrexone — is recovery. Alder will never suggest otherwise.

Privacy

Common questions

FAQ

Is Alder therapy?

No. Alder does not provide therapy, counseling, or clinical care, and using it doesn't create a professional relationship with anyone. It's a self-help companion that keeps your sober time, sits with you through hard moments, and points you toward real support. If you're working with a professional, Alder is meant to sit alongside that — never instead of it.

Am I talking to a real person?

No. Alder is designed to be an AI companion — not a therapist, sponsor, or doctor — and to say so plainly: a standing label sits at the top of the chat screen in the prototype today, and repeat disclosure over time is still to be built. Right now there is no AI behind it at all; the prototype's replies are scripted. Either way, nobody is reading your messages as you send them. That's the privacy trade: it also means Alder can't call for help on your behalf, which is why the phone numbers are always one tap away.

What happens if I relapse?

You get a debrief, not a lecture. Alder asks what happened, what led up to it, and what you'd want to do differently — and the time you'd already put in keeps counting toward your total across all attempts. Never being shamed for a lapse is a hard rule in this product, not a nice-to-have.

Can I track more than one thing?

Yes, and this was deliberate. Alcohol, substances, vaping, gambling and more can each have their own clock, so a hard week in one area doesn't wipe out what you've built in another.

Will it tell me how to detox or taper?

Never, for anything. Alder gives no detox, tapering, or dosing guidance of any kind. Stopping some substances abruptly — alcohol among them — can be genuinely dangerous, and that is a conversation for a medical professional, not an app. Alder will tell you that and help you find someone.

Does it work with medication-assisted recovery?

Yes, and it takes a clear position: buprenorphine, methadone, and naltrexone are recovery. Alder will never treat medication as cheating, and will never nudge you away from it.

Is my data private?

There's no account, no name, and no email, and your recovery record is kept on your device. There are no ads and no third-party analytics, and we don't sell or share anything. When conversations with Alder go live, your messages will be sent to a named AI provider to generate replies — you'll be told exactly who, and asked to agree, before a single message is sent. Full detail is in the privacy policy. What we won't do is claim a kind of legal confidentiality this app doesn't have.

What does it cost?

Nothing is for sale yet, so there's no price to quote. Whatever happens later, the SOS screen and the crisis numbers will never be metered, limited, or put behind a payment.

When can I use it?

Alder is in development. It's iPhone-first, US-first, and 18+. Email us and we'll tell you when there's something real to try.

Who is Alder for?

Adults 18 and over who want private support in between the humans in their life — whether you're in a program, in treatment with professionals, going to meetings, or doing this on your own for now.
