Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-09-22

This policy explains what Rowan keeps on your device, what reaches our backend and AI providers, and how to control it. Your messages can include sensitive health information. AI chat sends your message, recent conversation history and saved health context to Anthropic, with OpenAI as an automatic fallback. The independent safety check also uses those providers. Microphone transcription sends your recording to OpenAI. These are separate from the local tracking and planning tools.

Who we are

Rowan is an iOS app from HyperSage AI Labs LLC. There is no account and no login. A random identifier generated on your device connects your requests to the records on our server. This does not make the content anonymous: the name you choose, your messages and your health details may identify you. Contact: support@hypersage.ai.

The short version

  • You choose whether to use AI. The AI data-sharing disclosure names the information and providers before you allow sharing. You can use the local tools without AI, or turn off AI sharing later.
  • Anthropic and OpenAI can receive health information. Both can process text for replies and the independent safety check. OpenAI also processes a voice recording when you choose microphone transcription.
  • Conversations and some symptom logs are stored on our server. Your saved profile, tracking record, patterns and visit brief are managed on your device. Selected profile and symptom details are nevertheless sent with AI messages.
  • We do not sell your data or share it for advertising. We share the information described here with service providers to operate Rowan. There are no advertising SDKs or third-party product analytics attached to your health content.
  • You can export and delete your data. Deletion removes our active database records; existing database backups expire within five days. AI providers have their own retention rules for information already received.

What is sent off your device — and why

1. AI replies and the independent safety check

When you send an AI message, including an optional intake or an explicit "Ask Rowan" action, the app sends it to our backend with your locale and selected saved context:

  • Your preferred name and the saved notes under "What Rowan knows".
  • Conditions you marked as diagnosed or suspected.
  • Medication names, schedules and start dates. The separate medication dose field is not automatically attached, although anything you write in a message can be sent.
  • Allergies and their recorded reactions.
  • Recent symptom names, dates, severity and selected factors.

For a reply, the backend adds recent messages from the same stored conversation, up to about 14,000 characters. This can include earlier health details and Rowan's replies. Anthropic (Claude) is the primary reply provider. If its request fails to start, automatic fallback can send the same message, history and profile context to OpenAI. You are not asked again on each fallback; permission covers both named providers.

The independent safety check can separately send your current message, up to six recent conversation messages and your registered conditions to Anthropic, or to OpenAI if that check falls back. OpenAI can therefore receive this safety context even when Anthropic generates the reply. A model may also receive the arguments and results of actions it proposes during the conversation, such as logging a symptom. This check helps route responses and emergency resources; it is not human monitoring or an emergency service.

Your random device identifier and App Store subscription identifiers go to our backend for record ownership, usage limits and entitlement checks. They are not included in the AI provider requests. Free-form messages and recordings can still contain identifying information you choose to include.

2. Local tools and memory notes

Your tracking views, patterns, monthly recap and visit brief are assembled on your device. They do not trigger a separate cloud analysis. If you choose to discuss a finding with Rowan, the resulting AI message follows the sharing rules above.

Your phone holds the saved list of "What Rowan knows". Our server does not store a separate copy of that saved list. The list is, however, sent with each AI message and processed by our backend and the AI provider. Removing a saved note stops that note being attached on later turns; it does not erase an earlier conversation containing the same information, an existing backup or a provider's retained request.

Photos attached to log entries stay in Rowan's local record. On iPhone, Add a photo opens the system picker so you can choose an image or use the camera. Rowan receives only the image you choose; it does not browse or request broad access to your photo library. It stores a copy in its private app storage and shows it with the entry and visit brief. These attachments are not sent to Rowan's backend or AI providers.

The JSON data export contains attachment references, not the photo files; visit-brief exports also exclude the images and say so. Show a photo in the app or share a separate copy yourself. Removing an attachment or its entry can leave the local file until cleanup on a later app launch, so Undo can still work. Delete everything also removes Rowan's stored photo copies. It does not delete originals in your photo library or copies you have shared elsewhere.

3. Microphone transcription and read-aloud

Microphone transcription is optional. The AI-sharing disclosure explains the OpenAI recording transfer before you allow AI sharing. If sharing is off, choosing the microphone opens that disclosure first. After allowing AI sharing, you still choose when to record by tapping the microphone; iOS separately asks for microphone permission. There is no second Rowan audio-permission dialog, no background listening and no wake word. You can refuse microphone access or revoke it in iOS Settings and continue typing.

When you stop recording, the clip goes through our backend to OpenAI's transcription API. That provider receives the recording, which may itself contain health or identifying information. Your profile, saved notes and conversation history are not attached to the transcription request. Device and subscription identifiers are used by our backend, not passed to OpenAI with the audio.

The recording is held temporarily for transcription; Rowan does not save an audio file or put the recording in its database. The returned text goes into the message box for you to review and edit. It is not added to a conversation until you press Send. A transcription request can still leave usage counters and operational request metadata even if you never send that text. Once sent, the text is an ordinary AI message with the processing and retention described here. A recording is limited to about one minute.

Read-aloud uses your device's speech synthesis. Rowan does not send the reply text to our backend or an AI provider to speak it. The backend's cloud text-to-speech capability is disabled. Enabling cloud read-aloud would require a separate disclosure and permission for that use.

What we store and how long

Our database holds conversations and their messages, symptom logs created through the conversation, content-free safety events, usage counters and some subscription ownership records. A conversation title uses the first few words of its opening message. Records are linked to the device identifier, or to the separate pseudonymous App Store account token for subscription claims. There is no sign-in or cross-device account sync.

  • Conversations and messages: eligible for deletion after 90 days without a new message in that thread. Continuing a thread keeps its older messages until the whole thread has been inactive for 90 days.
  • Server symptom logs: 365 days from when the server stored them.
  • Safety events: 90 days. These contain a hashed device identifier, time, tier and bounded route/verdict/reason codes, not your message, symptom or condition text.
  • Usage and rate-limit counters: 35 days. These contain a day, resource and count keyed to a hashed device or network address, not health content.
  • Subscription ownership claims: an Apple transaction identifier, pseudonymous app-account token and creation time. These have no automatic expiry, to prevent reuse of the same receipt by another installation. The app's data-deletion request removes claims associated with its app-account token.

Expired rows are removed by a sweep scheduled every six hours. Database backups have a five-day retention period. A deleted record can remain in an existing backup until that backup expires; deleting active records does not rewrite those snapshots. Your on-device record remains until you remove it using the app's controls. These periods describe our database, not the separate AI-provider policies below.

AI-provider handling and retention

We use the providers' API services, not their public chat products. Their published API policies say inputs and outputs are not used for model training by default; opt-in and other stated exceptions are governed by those policies. We do not promise that every provider request is deleted immediately or within an unconditional 30-day maximum.

  • Anthropic: its standard API policy deletes inputs and outputs within 30 days, with exceptions including policy enforcement, legal requirements, certain features and separate agreements. Its published policy also describes longer retention for flagged content. API retention; model-training policy.
  • OpenAI: chat API abuse-monitoring logs can include request content and are normally retained for up to 30 days, with legal exceptions. Its current endpoint table lists no abuse-monitoring or application-state retention for the audio transcription endpoint Rowan uses. These are provider policies, not a claim that Rowan controls deletion inside OpenAI. API data controls and endpoint retention.

Deleting data in Rowan or withdrawing AI permission does not recall requests already sent or directly delete records held by an AI provider. Contact us if you need help with a request about that processing.

Protection and operational data

Connections from Rowan to our backend and from the backend to AI providers use HTTPS/TLS. Our database runs on Fly.io's private encrypted network, and its storage volume is encrypted at rest. Provider credentials remain on the server. Database reads, exports and deletes are scoped to the requesting device's identifier. This is not end-to-end encryption: our backend and the AI provider must process readable content to provide the requested feature.

We minimize operational logs and do not intentionally log message or audio bodies. Service logs can include connection metadata, timestamps, status codes and error details; they are separate from the content-free safety-event table. Third-party crash reporting to Sentry is disabled.

Rowan does not request access to your contacts, calendar, precise location, Apple Health record or iPhone medication list. Its optional photo picker handles only the image you select, as described above. Information you type, record or add to Rowan can include those details. We do not require an email address or account credentials, and do not use an advertising identifier.

Rowan is a consumer wellness app; we make no claim that it is a HIPAA-covered service. If a breach requires notification under applicable law, we will provide the required notices. Because Rowan does not require contact details, in-app and public notices may be necessary.

Your choices and rights

  • AI sharing: review the disclosure under Settings › About AI › AI data sharing. Choose to use Rowan without AI or allow sharing with the named providers. Turn off AI sharing under Settings › Privacy to stop later chat and transcription requests. Withdrawal does not undo processing already started or delete previous records.
  • Memory notes: view, remove an individual note or clear the saved list under "What Rowan knows". Earlier conversations are separate records.
  • Export: the app creates a JSON file containing its local record and the server rows tied to its identifiers, including any associated subscription ownership claims. If the server is unavailable, the export says so and includes only the local copy.
  • Delete: the app's delete-all control erases the local record and asks the backend to delete the associated conversations, messages, symptom logs, safety events, device usage counters and subscription claims. Successful server deletion returns a per-table receipt; if the service cannot be reached, the app reports that only the local copy was erased. A counter keyed to a network-address hash is not tied to one device and is not included; it expires under the 35-day rule. Existing backups and provider records follow the separate limits above. Removing the app alone does not send a server deletion request.
  • Privacy requests: you can use the in-app controls or contact support@hypersage.ai to request access, correction, export or deletion, or to withdraw consent. Applicable rights may depend on where you live. We rely on explicit consent for AI processing of health information and use limited operational data to secure and operate the service. We do not sell personal information or share it for cross-context advertising.
