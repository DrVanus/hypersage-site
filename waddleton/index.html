The island

Seven places to be. One of them has no name.

Every area is a painting, not a tileset — and each one has doors, games, and penguins standing around in it. Walk between them, or take the map.

Plus the mountain peak, and seven interiors to step inside — the aquarium, the arcade, a coffee house, a restaurant, a lounge, a club, and the loft above it.

The arcade

Fourteen games. Most have a door.

Ten of them live behind a sign out on the island — you walk up to the dock, the trailhead, the fort, and go in. The rest are cabinets you stand at inside the Floe Arcade. (If you'd rather not walk, the map carries a directory that will take you straight to any of the fourteen.) Snowflakes you earn playing them buy hats you'll wear back outside.

  • Every game keeps your personal best on the device you played it on.
  • Play together — start a party and launch a game as a group.

Together

Other penguins are actually other people.

Waddleton is a live world. The penguins wandering past you are players, and the island is better when it's busy.

Chat and emotes

Speech bubbles over your head, a wheel of emotes, and a quick-chat list of ready-made phrases if you'd rather tap than type.

Friends and parties

Add someone you actually met on the island, then start a party with a six-character code and take a game on together.

Snowballs

Throwable at anyone, harmless to everyone. The island's universal greeting.

Your own igloo

Decorate it with furniture, wallpaper, and flooring you've bought — drag every piece where you want it, turn it, and change the whole room whenever you like.

Seven pets to adopt

Adopt a fluffy one, feed it, play with it. It's a gentle loop — nothing bad ever happens to your pet. (An eighth is drawn and waiting, but it's priced in Goldfish, so nobody can adopt it yet.)

Badges and levels

Eighteen badges, an XP track, a daily bonus that rewards showing up, and a weekly rotation of limited items.

Straight answers

Free, and honest about what that means.

Games for kids have a long history of being sold as free. Here's exactly what Waddleton is.

No ads. Nothing to buy.

There are no advertisements in Waddleton and no in-app purchases — you cannot spend money here, because there's no way to. Nearly all of the shop — 135 of the 154 items — is bought with Snowflakes you earned by playing. The other 19 are priced in Goldfish or need a Waddleton Club membership, and since neither can be obtained today, those are simply not for sale to anyone.

No sign-up, and no tracking.

You don't create an account or hand over an email. There are no third-party analytics and no ad networks in the game at all. Your penguin is a name you picked and a set of choices, nothing more.

Your penguin lives in this browser.

That's the trade for not making you sign up: your progress is saved to the device and browser you're playing on. Your phone and your laptop are two different penguins, and clearing your browser data starts you over.

Common questions

FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes, completely. There are no ads and there is nothing you can buy — no purchases exist in the game. Snowflakes are earned by playing and buy 135 of the shop's 154 items. To be exact about the rest: 15 are priced in Goldfish, a premium currency that only a purchase would grant, and 4 need a "Waddleton Club" membership that hasn't been built. There is currently no way to get either, so those 19 items aren't purchasable by anybody — they're visible in the shop and that's all.

Do I have to make an account?

No. Open the page and you're a penguin. A guest identity is created for you automatically so other players can see you moving around the island, but there's no email, no password, and nothing to sign up for.

Will my penguin follow me to another device?

Not right now — and we'd rather say so than let you find out later. Your penguin is saved in the browser you're playing in, so your phone and your computer are two separate penguins, and there's no way to recover a penguin if you clear your browser data.

Is there an iPhone or Android app?

Not yet. Waddleton isn't on the App Store or Google Play. It runs in the browser on phones and tablets, and you can add it to your home screen if you want it to feel like an app.

Is the chat safe?

There's a quick-chat list of safe ready-made phrases if you'd rather not type at all, and you can mute or block any player and report anyone who's being a problem. To be straight with you: reports are recorded but there is no full-time moderation team reading them, so please use mute and block — they work immediately.

Are the leaderboards legit?

Not yet, and you deserve to know before you grind for a rank. The global boards are not live: the database side was never switched on, so nothing you score is posted anywhere and nothing you see on the LEADERBOARDS screen came from another player. Those rival names and scores are generated on your own device — practice targets, not people. What is real is your personal best in each game, which is saved on the device you played on and is genuinely yours. Real global boards are still the plan; we'll say so plainly when they work.

What do you collect about me?

Very little: the display name you chose, your penguin's appearance and progress, your scores, and — if you report someone — what you wrote in the report. No email, no real name, no location, no advertising identifiers, no analytics. The details are in the privacy policy.

Is Waddleton finished?

No, and that's the fun part. Areas, games, items, and events are still being added — the island in front of you today is smaller than the one that'll be there in a few months.
