Privacy

Last updated 5 October 2026

The short version

Holohound has no accounts and no sign-in. In the iPhone app, your collection is stored on your device, not on our servers. We run no analytics, no advertising and no third-party tracking. Your complete binder is not uploaded. If you allow Hound data sharing and send a message, relevant card details and a binder summary can leave your device, as described below. Our server also handles messages to Hound, the AI companion; sold-price lookups for a card; and, if you subscribe, proof of your Holohound Pro subscription. Hound's replies are written by Anthropic's AI.

What stays on your device

Your binder, what you paid, your want list, your price watches, conversations, and the value history the app records over time live in the app's own storage on your device. Selected information can be sent when you use Hound, as described below. Deleting the app can remove this local data.

When you scan a card, the app also keeps a rectified photo of it on your device, capped at your newest 40 captures so the library can't grow without bound. These photos are never uploaded anywhere; the card-matching step that reads them runs entirely on the device.

Profile → Collection backup lets you save a separate JSON backup and preview a backup before explicitly replacing your collection. A backup includes collection lots, costs, sales, tags, wish list, pending scans, preferences, conversations, value and centering history, your profile image, and card descriptions for offline recovery. Camera captures, native photos, device identity, Hound allowance and Pro purchases are excluded. Use Restore Purchases for Pro.

The exported JSON file is not encrypted; store and share it only somewhere you trust. iCloud sync and automatic cloud backup are unavailable in this build. We cannot restore your collection from our servers. Keep a separate backup if you want to recover it after losing the device or deleting the app. Deleting the app does not delete backup files you saved elsewhere.

What leaves your device

Card images and card data

Card artwork and card details are fetched as you browse, from a different catalogue for each game: TCGdex for Pokémon, Scryfall for Magic, YGOPRODeck for Yu-Gi-Oh!, Lorcast for Lorcana, and gcg-api for Gundam. Like any request to any website, whichever catalogue you are browsing receives your IP address and can see which cards were requested. Yu-Gi-Oh! and Gundam card images are fetched through our own server, which requests that card's image on the app's behalf, so the image host sees our server rather than your device. We do not send any catalogue your collection, and these requests carry no identifier from us.

Holohound also links out to graders and marketplaces — PSA, Beckett, CGC, SGC, TCGplayer, eBay and Cardmarket — when you tap a certificate, product or search link. A marketplace search includes the card name, set and number and, when specified, its finish, language or grade. Search links open a search page; they do not mean Holohound has fetched or verified a seller's offer. Those sites receive the search terms and your normal browser request, including your IP address, and are covered by their own privacy policies. Constructing the link sends nothing to the marketplace; it is opened only when you tap.

Prices

Every price in the app names the feed it came from, on the card itself, because the feeds are not the same and do not mean the same thing. Pokémon: TCGplayer market prices in US dollars and Cardmarket prices in euro, both supplied through TCGdex, never converted into one another. Magic: TCGplayer prices in dollars, supplied through Scryfall. Yu-Gi-Oh!: TCGplayer's lowest-printing figure, supplied through YGOPRODeck — a floor, not a market price. Lorcana: Lorcast, in dollars; Lorcast names no marketplace behind its numbers, so neither do we. Gundam: no price feed exists yet, so the app shows no price at all rather than an invented one. Holohound is not endorsed or certified by TCGplayer, Cardmarket, TCGdex, Scryfall, YGOPRODeck, Lorcast or gcg-api.

When you open a card's price chart, the app asks our server for the daily prices it has recorded for that product. That request carries the product number and, like any web request, your IP address — nothing else. The app also fetches a small settings file from our server that carries nothing about you.

Sold-price lookups

When its sold-listings feed is connected, the app can show recent sold prices for a card, including graded sales. That feed is not always connected and does not cover every card, and when the app cannot get sold prices for a card it says so on that card. To look it up, the app sends our server the card's catalogue id, its name, number and printing, the grading company and grade if the card is graded, the random install id described below, and — if you subscribe to Pro — the Apple-signed record of your subscription. Our server counts lookups per IP address per day, so one network cannot run up unlimited lookups, and asks our sold-listings data provider (currently Scrydex, which supplies eBay sold-listing data) for that card. The provider is sent the card, not your install id, your IP address or your collection.

Hound, the AI companion

Before the first message is sent, the app asks you to allow Hound data sharing and names Anthropic, the AI provider. If you allow it and send a message, our server receives and forwards the following to Anthropic to generate the reply:

  • The message you type; any photos you deliberately attach (up to three per message, reduced to 1,024 pixels on the long edge); and the last eight messages of the conversation.
  • The card you opened Hound from, including its game, name, set, number, quantity, condition, recorded finish and language, what you paid, recorded grading details, and centering measurements when available.
  • Price references, including amount, currency, source, quote type, printing coverage, the source-provided date and the app's separate price-check time when available. Missing dates or prices remain unknown.
  • A binder summary: counts, value, cost and cost coverage, your eight most valuable priced cards, and progress on up to six sets. A question about a set may also include the cards you are missing from it. Your complete collection is not sent.
  • Offers you choose to ask Hound about: seller labels, currency, item and delivery costs, taxes or fees, quantities, and whether you confirmed that the card details match. Calculated comparison totals may be included so Hound can explain them.

Comparing entered offers on your device sends nothing to AI. Asking Hound about a comparison prepares a message; you still choose whether to send it. These are figures you entered, not fetched listings. Avoid entering personal or sensitive information in seller labels or messages.

Our server also receives catalogue and holding identifiers to associate the context with the selected card, your random install id, and, if you have Pro, your App Store subscription record for allowance and subscription checks. Those identifiers and the subscription record are not included as metadata in the request to Anthropic. We do not store the conversation on our servers. Anthropic's commercial data-retention policy describes its handling of API inputs and outputs, including its standard deletion period and exceptions for safety, legal requirements or other agreed retention. Its retention is separate from the conversation stored on your device.

You can change your choice in Profile → About & legal → Hound data sharing. Choosing Not now prevents new Hound messages from being sent; scanning, your binder and price references keep working. Changing the choice does not recall a request already sent or delete data already received by Anthropic.

The install id and daily counts

The app generates a random id for your install. It is not a login, and it is not your Apple ID or an advertising identifier. It is sent with every Hound message and every sold-price lookup, and our server uses it, together with the IP address the request arrives from, to keep daily counts: how many Hound messages an install and a network have sent that day, and how many sold-price lookups a network has made. The allowances reset at midnight UTC. The counts are tallies against those ids and addresses; they are not joined to your collection, and the app ships no password, no email address and no profile.

Holohound Pro

If you subscribe, Holohound Pro is sold and billed by Apple through the App Store. We never see your name, your Apple ID, your card or your billing address. When you subscribe, Apple gives the app a signed record of the transaction, and the app sends that record with each Hound message and sold-price lookup, so our server can check against Apple's certificate that the subscription is real and current before applying Pro's limits.

To stop one subscription being shared across strangers' phones, our server keeps a one-way hash of the subscription's original transaction id, together with up to five install ids that have used it and when each last did. An install id that has not used the subscription for 30 days stops occupying one of those five seats. The service checks for expired seats across stored subscriptions during startup and request handling, at most once per hour during normal operation; removal therefore depends on service activity and a successful storage write. This seat cleanup does not imply deletion of usage, purchase-verification, refund or other records described in this policy. To ask about or request removal of server-held information, email us. For a subscriber, the install id and its daily Hound count are linked on our server to the subscription.

What we never collect

  • No name, email address or phone number — there is nothing to sign up for.
  • No payment details — Apple handles every purchase.
  • No advertising identifiers, no ad networks, no cross-app tracking.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDK is present in this build. If something breaks, Profile offers a "Share diagnostics" report — it is assembled on your device, you can read every line before sharing it, and the app never transmits it anywhere itself.
  • No location, contacts, or microphone access is requested, and no photo-library permission: a photo you choose to attach is picked with the system photo picker, which hands the app only that photo.

Camera

The app requests camera access to identify the card in front of it — asked for in context, the moment you first tap Scan, never on launch. Recognition runs entirely on your device against an index of card images bundled with the app: nothing the camera sees while you are scanning is uploaded, and no video ever leaves the device.

The one exception, and it is yours to make: if you deliberately attach a photo to a message to Hound, that photo is sent with the message, exactly as described above. Nothing attaches a photo on your behalf.

Children

Holohound is not directed at children under 13, and its App Store age rating will be set by Apple from our content questionnaire when the app lists. It asks no one for a name, an email address or contact details, and it has no social features and no chat with other people. Hound is an AI, not a person and not another user; what a message to it sends is listed in full above.

Your choices

You can export your collection to a CSV file or save a Collection backup from the Profile screen without a subscription. A CSV is a collection export; use Collection backup when you want the additional preferences, conversations and history described above. Deleting the app can remove your local collection, scan photos and install id, but it does not remove exported files from wherever you saved or shared them. If you subscribed, cancel Holohound Pro in your Apple ID's subscription settings — deleting the app does not cancel it — and the install stops counting toward the subscription's five places 30 days after it was last used, as described above. For a question about anything our server holds, or to ask for it to be removed, email us.
