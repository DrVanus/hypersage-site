Holohound

Privacy

Last updated 13 September 2026

The short version

Holohound has no accounts and no sign-in. In the iPhone app, your collection is stored on your device, not on our servers. We run no analytics, no advertising and no third-party tracking. We cannot see your binder. Three things do reach our own server, and each is described below: messages to Hound, the AI companion; sold-price lookups for a card; and, if you subscribe, proof of your Holohound Pro subscription. Hound's replies are written by Anthropic's AI.

What stays on your device

Everything you create in the app: the cards in your binder, what you paid, your want list, your price watches, and the value history the app records over time. This lives in the app's own storage on your device. Deleting the app deletes it.

When you scan a card, the app also keeps a rectified photo of it on your device, capped at your newest 40 captures so the library can't grow without bound. These photos are never uploaded anywhere; the card-matching step that reads them runs entirely on the device.

Because it is only on your device, we cannot restore it for you if you lose the device or delete the app. Backup is not implemented in this version.

What leaves your device

Card images and card data

Card artwork and card details are fetched as you browse, from a different catalogue for each game: TCGdex for Pokémon, Scryfall for Magic, YGOPRODeck for Yu-Gi-Oh!, Lorcast for Lorcana, and gcg-api for Gundam. Like any request to any website, whichever catalogue you are browsing receives your IP address and can see which cards were requested. Yu-Gi-Oh! and Gundam card images are fetched through our own server, which requests that card's image on the app's behalf, so the image host sees our server rather than your device. We do not send any catalogue your collection, and these requests carry no identifier from us.

Holohound also links out to graders and marketplaces — PSA, Beckett, CGC, SGC, TCGplayer and eBay — when you tap a certificate or a listing. Those open outside the app and are covered by their own privacy policies, not this one. Nothing is sent to them unless you tap.

Prices

Every price in the app names the feed it came from, on the card itself, because the feeds are not the same and do not mean the same thing. Pokémon: TCGplayer market prices in US dollars and Cardmarket prices in euro, both supplied through TCGdex, never converted into one another. Magic: TCGplayer prices in dollars, supplied through Scryfall. Yu-Gi-Oh!: TCGplayer's lowest-printing figure, supplied through YGOPRODeck — a floor, not a market price. Lorcana: Lorcast, in dollars; Lorcast names no marketplace behind its numbers, so neither do we. Gundam: no price feed exists yet, so the app shows no price at all rather than an invented one. Holohound is not endorsed or certified by TCGplayer, Cardmarket, TCGdex, Scryfall, YGOPRODeck, Lorcast or gcg-api.

When you open a card's price chart, the app asks our server for the daily prices it has recorded for that product. That request carries the product number and, like any web request, your IP address — nothing else. The app also fetches a small settings file from our server that carries nothing about you.

Sold-price lookups

When its sold-listings feed is connected, the app can show recent sold prices for a card, including graded sales. That feed is not always connected and does not cover every card, and when the app cannot get sold prices for a card it says so on that card. To look it up, the app sends our server the card's catalogue id, its name, number and printing, the grading company and grade if the card is graded, the random install id described below, and — if you subscribe to Pro — the Apple-signed record of your subscription. Our server counts lookups per IP address per day, so one network cannot run up unlimited lookups, and asks our sold-listings data provider (currently Scrydex, which supplies eBay sold-listing data) for that card. The provider is sent the card, not your install id, your IP address or your collection.

Hound, the AI companion

When you send Hound a message, this is exactly what is sent, and nothing more: the message you type, any photo you attach to it, the last few messages of that conversation, and the specific card rows relevant to your question — which can include what you paid for a card and its condition. They go to our server and are forwarded to Anthropic, whose AI model writes the reply. Your whole collection is not sent, no account is created, and we do not store the conversation on our servers. Anthropic retains API inputs and outputs for a limited period under its trust-and-safety policy before deleting them; that retention is theirs, not ours. None of it is used for tracking.

The install id and daily counts

The app generates a random id for your install. It is not a login, and it is not your Apple ID or an advertising identifier. It is sent with every Hound message and every sold-price lookup, and our server uses it, together with the IP address the request arrives from, to keep daily counts: how many Hound messages an install and a network have sent that day, and how many sold-price lookups a network has made. The allowances reset at midnight UTC. The counts are tallies against those ids and addresses; they are not joined to your collection, and the app ships no password, no email address and no profile.

Holohound Pro

If you subscribe, Holohound Pro is sold and billed by Apple through the App Store. We never see your name, your Apple ID, your card or your billing address. When you subscribe, Apple gives the app a signed record of the transaction, and the app sends that record with each Hound message and sold-price lookup, so our server can check against Apple's certificate that the subscription is real and current before applying Pro's limits.

To stop one subscription being shared across strangers' phones, our server keeps a one-way hash of the subscription's original transaction id, together with up to five install ids that have used it and when each last did. An install id that has not used the subscription for 30 days stops counting toward those five, but it can stay in the record until the subscription is next used, and the record itself has no automatic deletion date yet; to ask us to remove it, email us. This means that, for a subscriber, the install id and its daily Hound count are linked on our server to the subscription.

What we never collect

  • No name, email address or phone number — there is nothing to sign up for.
  • No payment details — Apple handles every purchase.
  • No advertising identifiers, no ad networks, no cross-app tracking.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDK is present in this build. If something breaks, Profile offers a "Share diagnostics" report — it is assembled on your device, you can read every line before sharing it, and the app never transmits it anywhere itself.
  • No location, contacts, or microphone access is requested, and no photo-library permission: a photo you choose to attach is picked with the system photo picker, which hands the app only that photo.

Camera

The app requests camera access to identify the card in front of it — asked for in context, the moment you first tap Scan, never on launch. Recognition runs entirely on your device against an index of card images bundled with the app: nothing the camera sees while you are scanning is uploaded, and no video ever leaves the device.

The one exception, and it is yours to make: if you deliberately attach a photo to a message to Hound, that photo is sent with the message, exactly as described above. Nothing attaches a photo on your behalf.

Children

Holohound is not directed at children under 13, and its App Store age rating will be set by Apple from our content questionnaire when the app lists. It asks no one for a name, an email address or contact details, and it has no social features and no chat with other people. Hound is an AI, not a person and not another user; what a message to it sends is listed in full above.

Your choices

You can export your collection to a CSV file from the Profile screen at any time, without a subscription. Deleting the app erases your collection, your scan photos and the install id on your device. If you subscribed, cancel Holohound Pro in your Apple ID's subscription settings — deleting the app does not cancel it — and the install stops counting toward the subscription's five places 30 days after it was last used, as described above. For a question about anything our server holds, or to ask for it to be removed, email us.

Changes

If what the app does changes, this page changes on the same day. The date at the top is the honest one.
