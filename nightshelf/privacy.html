Nightshelf

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 26, 2026

Nightshelf ("the app", "we", "us") is a bedtime-story app for iOS. It is built to know as little about you as possible. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics SDK, no third-party tracker, and no advertising. This policy explains the little that's left.

What stays on your device

  • Your shelf and progress — which stories you've opened, your place in each one, and your bedtime settings (voice, speed, sleep timer, ambient sound). These live on your device and, via Apple's private iCloud key-value storage, sync between your own devices. Nightshelf keeps no copy of them on any server.
  • On-device narration — the built-in iOS voice synthesizes every word locally. Nothing leaves your phone, and it never needs a connection.
  • Nova's welcome — from Nightshelf 1.9.3, a small record on this iPhone of how much of Nova's welcome you've heard: how many characters Nova has read, when the welcome began and ended and why, and when it may try again if Nova couldn't be reached. It is kept in this iPhone's Keychain, so deleting and reinstalling Nightshelf does not play the welcome again. It is not copied to your other devices or to a new iPhone, and it is never sent to our server. Nightshelf Pro does not use it.

What leaves your device, and why

From Nightshelf 1.9.3, the books that come with Nightshelf can be read by an AI narrator without asking first: the paragraph being read and the one after it go to Nightshelf's server, which asks OpenAI to voice them. OpenAI gets only that text, the narrator's name and a reading-style note — no ID, and nothing you typed. Nightshelf's server sees your internet address, as any website does, and keeps a scrambled daily character count to limit use. You can turn this off in Settings → AI features. Tonight's Story and the stories you made are different: Nightshelf asks before anything you choose or type leaves your phone. In Nightshelf 1.9.2 and earlier, every cloud narration asks first as well, including the books that come with Nightshelf; once allowed, it sends the same text. The details are below, along with the one other thing that goes out without asking.

  • AI narrators reading Nightshelf's books (from Nightshelf 1.9.3) — the six AI narrators are Nova, Alloy, Fable, Onyx, Echo and Shimmer. With Nightshelf Pro, the one you choose reads, or Nova when your voice is set to Automatic. Without Pro, Nova reads your first few minutes of Nightshelf's books once on each iPhone, when your voice is set to Automatic; then, at the end of a paragraph, your iPhone's own voice carries on, offline. Either way, the paragraph being read is sent to be turned into audio, and so is the paragraph after it, fetched early so the reading doesn't pause. Requests for Nova's welcome carry no device ID and no receipt; a Pro request carries the ID and receipt described below. Settings → AI features → AI narrators for Nightshelf's books turns this off, and your iPhone's voice then reads every book that comes with Nightshelf, offline. The switch syncs with your other settings through your iCloud. If you said no to cloud narration on this iPhone in an earlier version, the switch starts off. No book text is sent for narration when you have chosen a particular iPhone voice, when AI narrators are off, or, without Pro, once Nova's minutes are done. If your iPhone's voice is only standing in for an AI narrator that was interrupted, the paragraphs ahead can still be sent, so the narrator can pick the story back up.
  • Tonight's Story and stories you made — writing a custom bedtime story sends the picks you made from the lists — setting, tone, weather, time of night and length — plus the optional detail you typed. If a cover picture is painted for it, the story's title, tone and setting are sent so it can be drawn. Having an AI narrator read a story you made sends its paragraphs the same way as a book. Nightshelf asks your permission the first time, and if you say no, none of this is sent, then or later, unless you change your answer in Settings → AI features. Stories you made still read aloud in your iPhone's voice. Please don't include personal details you wouldn't want sent.
  • Auditioning a narrator. Tapping ▶ beside a narrator's name sends a single fixed sample sentence, the same one for every listener, written into the app itself. It travels the same road as everything else. We don't put it behind the permission because it cannot carry anything of yours — not the page you were reading, not a word you typed, not even which book you were in. It happens only when you tap ▶. With Nightshelf Pro, and in versions before 1.9.3, it also carries the random device or subscription ID described below.

Where it goes: three stops, not two

Everything travels the same road.

  • Nightshelf's own server first — storyvault-api.fly.dev, which we run and which is hosted for us by Fly.io in the United States. Your words reach us and our host before they reach anyone else.
  • OpenAI — turns text into audio for the AI narrators, and paints story covers. For narration it receives the text to be read, the narrator's name and a reading-style note, with no ID of yours.
  • Anthropic — its Claude model writes a Tonight's Story.

OpenAI and Anthropic retain text according to their own terms and applicable account arrangements. Nightshelf does not promise that either provider discards it immediately. What we can tell you is what we do, below.

What our server keeps

  • A device or subscription ID. Nightshelf has no account or sign-in and does not require your name or email. A story detail can contain identifying information. We send that text without removing identifying details, so please leave personal information out. Each cloud request also carries a random ID that stands in for your phone. From Nightshelf 1.9.3, requests from a listener without Nightshelf Pro — Nova's welcome and ▶ auditions — no longer carry it. The ID isn't made from anything about you, and deleting and reinstalling Nightshelf keeps the same one. If you subscribed in the app, a phone that picks up that subscription switches to the ID saved with the purchase instead, so your devices share that one ID. These records can be linked to your device or subscription, even though they do not carry your name or email. It exists so daily limits can be counted per device or subscription rather than per network — otherwise everyone behind one router would share a single limit. Counting requires keeping it: our server stores a scrambled (SHA-256) copy of that ID alongside the date, which feature was used, and a running number. No book title, no story, and none of your words are in that row. Rows more than 35 days old are deleted when the clean-up runs — but nothing runs it on a timer. It piggybacks on other traffic reaching the same server, so a row can sit there longer than 35 days, and we would rather say that than imply a schedule we haven't built.
  • A record for a subscription from an offer code. These subscriptions lack the app account token used by an in-app purchase, so our server keeps a separate record. It holds Apple's transaction IDs, the first presenting device's random ID stored as it is, and verification timestamps. A separate recent-device history keeps scrambled (SHA-256) copies of device IDs with first- and last-seen times, linked to the subscription. The history is bounded to recent devices; it is not an access limit. A device removed from that history can return with a current, valid verified receipt. The subscription's devices share one service budget. These records contain no name, email, card details or story text and are not part of the 35-day usage clean-up. Older history entries can be replaced as devices return, while the transaction record and first device's ID can remain after the subscription ends. To request release of the retained subscription record, email the address below.
  • In-progress usage reservations. While a cloud request is being handled, we reserve its allowance so simultaneous requests cannot spend the same allowance twice. The reservation stores a random reservation ID, a scrambled subscription or device budget key, the UTC date, the feature, a numeric amount and its creation time. It contains no receipt, raw device ID, prompt, story, audio or provider response. Completed or refunded reservations are deleted when settlement succeeds. If settlement fails or a worker stops, the amount remains counted for that UTC day. Remaining reservation rows older than 35 days are eligible for removal when a later admitted request runs the clean-up; there is no scheduled deletion deadline.
  • Audio, for a while. Narration we have already produced is kept in a shared cache, filed under a fingerprint of the paragraph rather than under any device — so the second listener to reach the first page of a book gets it without it being made again. The cached audio can contain identifying details from the words it reads; a fingerprint filename does not make it anonymous. Audio can remain until space is needed or the server's temporary storage is replaced. There is no fixed time limit.
  • Ordinary logs. Narration records which narrator read and how many characters were spoken. A Tonight's Story records the setting, tone and length — and, on some requests, the weather and time of night — plus whether you typed a detail at all, never the detail itself, and how many chapters came back. When the model returns something we cannot use, the failure line records the shape of what came back — which kinds of block, which fields were present — and none of its text. An offer-code subscription adds access-history events: adding a device to recent history can record the subscription's original Apple transaction ID and configured history limit; trimming older history records the number removed. Support release and verification refusal events can also include subscription identifiers. Normal success logs do not include the page, your typed detail, or the generated story and title. For cloud narration, story writing and cover generation, our provider-error logs now record error metadata — such as error type, status and provider request ID — instead of raw provider messages. Older logs may still contain provider errors and submitted content they echoed. This update does not erase those records or change provider retention policies.
  • Your IP address. Reaching our server shows it your IP, the way visiting a website does. The guest allowance uses a scrambled (SHA-256) copy of that IP. From Nightshelf 1.9.3, Nova's welcome is counted the same way: a daily character count kept against the scrambled IP, with no device ID. Web-server access logs can also contain the client IP address, request path and response status. The app also pings the server once at launch to check it is awake: in Nightshelf 1.9.2 and earlier when a cloud narrator is selected, and from 1.9.3 when AI narrators are on and one would read Nightshelf's books — with Nightshelf Pro, or while Nova's welcome is still open. That ping carries no text.

Purchases

Nightshelf Pro is bought through Apple. Apple handles the transaction and gives the app a signed receipt, so it knows you're a subscriber. The app sends that receipt to our server with its cloud requests so the server can check it; the receipt carries no name, email or card details. What the server keeps from it for a subscription redeemed with an offer code is described above. You can manage the subscription anytime in Settings → your name → Subscriptions.

Premium system voices

If you download Apple's Premium or Enhanced voices from iOS Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content (Read & Speak on newer iOS) → Voices, that download and those voices are handled by iOS, not by Nightshelf. From Nightshelf 1.9.2, Automatic reads with the best voice installed on your iPhone and switches by itself when you download a better one, and you can choose any installed voice. They all read on the phone, offline.

Data sharing

We do not sell data and we do not share anything with advertisers. Nothing described above is used to build a profile of you or to advertise to you.

Children's privacy

Nightshelf is made for adults who read themselves to sleep, and it is rated 13+. It is not directed to children and is not part of Apple's Kids Category. The shelf is adult literary fiction — alongside the fairy tales there is a Mystery & Gothic shelf holding Dracula, Jekyll & Hyde, Poe and Lovecraft — so parents should treat it the way they would a bookshelf, not a children's app.

No account or sign-in is required, and Nightshelf does not ask for your name or email address. Its cloud features process the text they read aloud or that you choose to send, and the identifiers described above. Please do not enter personal details about a child in a story prompt. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has sent us personal information through Nightshelf, email support@hypersage.ai so we can investigate and delete any such information we retain.
