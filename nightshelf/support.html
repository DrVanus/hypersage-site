Support

One developer reads every email. You'll get a real reply, usually within a day or two — support@hypersage.ai.

Do I need a connection?

Not for the library. All 93 classic books ship inside the app, and the on-device narrator reads them with no network at all — a plane, a basement, or airplane mode are all fine. Only the six cloud narrators and writing a new Tonight's Story need a connection.

Which books are free?

Nineteen of the ninety-three — among them The Wind in the Willows, Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, A Christmas Carol, Treasure Island, Black Beauty, Grimm, Aesop, Frankenstein, Journey to the Centre of the Earth, Meditations and Tales from Shakespeare. They stay free for as long as you keep the app, with no account and no ads. Nightshelf Pro opens the other seventy-four, plus the six cloud narrators, Tonight's Story, and sleep timers past an hour.

All ninety-three are installed either way — that is why the shelf works offline — but a locked book shows a padlock until you subscribe.

Where are the familiar fairy tales?

Open Familiar Tales on Home for twelve complete selections, including Snow White (the historical Snowdrop version), Cinderella and Sleeping Beauty. Their source books remain available too. Search also recognizes familiar alternate titles, including Snowdrop and The Princess and the Pea.

The 93 classic books, 11 AI-written Originals and 24 selected tales make 128 listening choices: 35 free and 93 with Pro. Selected tales come from the included classic books, so they are not counted as extra books. They have their own saved place and running time. Familiar Tales stops at the selected tale's ending; Short listens can continue to another eligible tale when continuation is enabled and the timer has time left.

The narrator sounds flat

That is your iPhone's own voice. From Nightshelf 1.9.2, whenever your iPhone's voice is reading, Automatic uses the best English voice installed on your iPhone and switches by itself when you download a better one, and you can choose any installed voice in the voice menu, free. For a warmer sound, download one: in iOS Settings, open Accessibility → Spoken Content (Read & Speak on newer iOS) → Voices → English, choose a voice marked Premium or Enhanced, and download it. Larger voices can be over 100 MB, so Wi-Fi helps. With Nightshelf Pro you can also choose one of the six AI narrators — Nova, Alloy, Fable, Onyx, Echo or Shimmer — for the whole night.

Nova read the first few minutes, then the voice changed

From Nightshelf 1.9.3, if you don't have Nightshelf Pro and your voice is set to Automatic, Nova, one of Nightshelf's AI narrators, reads your first few minutes of Nightshelf's books, once on each iPhone. Then, at the end of a paragraph, your iPhone's own voice carries on, offline, for as long as you like. The minutes are counted in text, not time, so a faster reading speed makes them shorter. Every paragraph sent for Nova to read counts, including the next one, which is fetched early so the reading doesn't pause. Reinstalling Nightshelf doesn't start them again. If you chose a particular iPhone voice, Nova doesn't read. You can switch to your iPhone's voice sooner from the Nova note in the player, and Settings → AI features → AI narrators for Nightshelf's books turns AI narrators off. Nightshelf Pro keeps Nova, or any of the six AI narrators, reading all night.

Nova needs a connection. If she can't be reached, your iPhone's voice reads instead, and the minutes she hasn't read are kept for later.

The story switched to the device voice

Cloud narration with Nightshelf Pro has no nightly allowance to run through. Its one ceiling is a fair-use limit, there to stop automated misuse, and a banner says when narration resumes if you ever meet it. A dropped connection or a service outage does the same thing: the story carries on in the on-device voice, and the cloud narrator comes back on its own — the book keeps reading either way.

If the banner says Pro couldn't be confirmed with the narrator, tap it to retry. If that keeps happening, use Restore Purchases and see Restoring Nightshelf Pro below.

It kept reading after I fell asleep

Set the sleep timer before you start. Narration fades out rather than stopping abruptly, so it does not jolt you awake. Your place in the book is saved either way, so you can pick up wherever you drifted off.

My progress did not follow to my other device

Progress syncs through your own private iCloud storage, so both devices need to be signed into the same Apple Account with iCloud Drive on. There is no Nightshelf account and no copy on any server of ours — with iCloud off, progress simply stays on the one device.

Tonight's Story will not generate

It needs a connection and takes a few seconds to write. If it keeps failing, check your connection and try a shorter prompt. If the app says tonight's stories are spent, that is the daily cap — a few fresh stories a day, then it resets. Every book already on your shelf still reads aloud in the meantime.

Restoring Nightshelf Pro

Open the Pro screen and tap Restore Purchases. If the Apple Account signed in to the App Store has an active subscription, Nightshelf Pro opens again — there is no account to log into. If the App Store cannot be reached, the app says so rather than telling you there is no subscription; try again once you are back online.

A subscription redeemed with an offer code can use the cloud narrators, Tonight's Story and story covers across your devices once its current Apple receipt is verified. All those devices share the subscription's service limits. There is no fixed five-device refusal. If access still cannot be confirmed, check that the subscription is active under the Apple Account used for Media & Purchases, use Restore Purchases, and tap the narrator banner to retry. If it persists, email us with the message shown; please do not send your receipt or any story details.

Refunds and billing

All purchases are handled by Apple. Request a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com, and manage or cancel in iOS Settings → your name → Subscriptions.

Reporting a bug

Email support@hypersage.ai with what you saw and what you were doing. Your device model and iOS version help a lot.

Where your data goes

The privacy policy has the full details. In short, the app has no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no third-party tracker and no advertising. Reading progress and settings live on your device and in your own private iCloud storage.

The on-device narrator speaks every word on the phone, so no book text is sent for narration while an iPhone voice you chose is reading. If it is only standing in for a cloud narrator that was interrupted, the paragraphs ahead can still be sent, so that narrator can pick the story back up. From Nightshelf 1.9.3, AI narrators can read the books that come with Nightshelf without asking first — Nova for your first few minutes on each iPhone, or all night with Nightshelf Pro — and they are on from the start, with Automatic as the starting voice. The paragraph being read and the one after it go to Nightshelf's own server (hosted for us by Fly.io in the United States) and then to OpenAI, which gets only that text, the narrator's name and a reading-style note, with no ID. Our server sees your IP address, as any website does; with Pro, the request also carries the ID and receipt described in the privacy policy. Settings → AI features → AI narrators for Nightshelf's books turns this off. If you said no when Nightshelf asked about its cloud features in an earlier version, on this iPhone or on an iPhone whose backup this one was restored from, the switch starts off, and because it syncs through iCloud, it is then off on your other devices too. Anything you choose or type is different: Tonight's Story, its cover, and an AI narrator reading a story you made all ask your permission first, and if you say no, none of it is sent. In Nightshelf 1.9.2 and earlier, every cloud narration asks first, including the books that come with Nightshelf. When Tonight's Story runs, your picks and detail go through Nightshelf's server to Anthropic, which writes the story, and a cover's title, tone and setting go to OpenAI to be painted.

Two smaller things go out without asking, and the privacy policy spells them out. Tapping ▶ to audition a narrator sends one fixed sample sentence — the same one for everyone, built into the app. It carries no page of a book and nothing you have typed, which is why it isn't gated, and it only happens when you tap ▶. Before Nightshelf 1.9.3 it carried the random device or subscription ID described there for every listener; from 1.9.3 it does so only with Nightshelf Pro. That ID is not anonymous: the records it keys can be linked to your device or subscription, even though they hold no name or email. The other is a check at launch that the server is awake, made when an AI narrator would read; it carries no text.

Our server keeps a scrambled copy of a random ID so daily limits can be counted per device or subscription. From Nightshelf 1.9.3, a listener without Pro sends no such ID: Nova's welcome and ▶ auditions are counted against a scrambled copy of the IP address instead, and the record of how much of Nova's welcome you've heard stays on your iPhone and is never sent. Each phone has its own ID and keeps it through a reinstall; a phone that picks up a subscription bought in the app switches to the ID saved with that purchase. A subscription redeemed with an offer code has no such ID, so the server keeps a separate record for it — Apple's transaction IDs, the first device's random ID, and a bounded recent-device history of scrambled device IDs and first- and last-seen times — which the privacy policy sets out in full. The server also keeps narration it has already made in a shared cache, filed under a fingerprint of the paragraph rather than under a device or subscription. No Nightshelf account or sign-in is required. Optional story details are sent as entered, so please leave personal information out.
